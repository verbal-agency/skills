# Practice design

## Evidence ladder

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| recognize | identify a valid instance among plausible alternatives |
| recall | produce a fact or relation without cues |
| explain | reconstruct meaning or mechanism in the learner's own structure |
| discriminate | distinguish confusable cases and justify the boundary |
| apply | use the concept in a bounded but new situation |
| diagnose | infer causes from symptoms and test alternatives |
| design | produce an artifact under explicit constraints and tradeoffs |
| defend | respond to objections, limits, and alternative designs |

Match the required level to the authentic capability. Do not require design
when competent application is the stated outcome, and do not accept recall
when diagnosis is required.

## Item construction

- State the task and constraints without embedding answer terminology.
- Make distractors correspond to plausible misconceptions, not trivia.
- Ask for reasoning when reasoning is part of the capability.
- Vary surface details across variants while preserving the tested structure.
- Use a separate scoring key containing expected evidence, partial credit,
  disqualifying errors, and feedback routes.
- Tag prerequisites so a wrong answer can be diagnosed rather than merely scored.

## Practice progression

Use retrieval before rereading. Follow a worked example with a completion
problem, then an independent problem with changed surface details. Add contrast,
failure, and transfer only when the target capability requires them. Delay some
retrieval to test retention rather than immediate imitation.

## Lab contract

Specify starting state, environment, cost and safety ceiling, task, observable
success, expected failure, hints ordered from strategic to specific, cleanup,
and a simulation or reasoning alternative when infrastructure is unavailable.
Require an artifact such as output, diff, diagram, benchmark, diagnosis, or
decision record.

## Leakage checks

Keep solutions out of learner-facing filenames and prompts. Do not reuse the
exact wording of the explanation as the only acceptable answer. During oral or
adaptive assessment, reveal one hint at a time and record hint level as part of
the evidence.
